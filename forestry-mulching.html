Forestry mulchingGroesbeck & Central Texas

Brush, cedar and small trees ground up where they stand. No burn piles, no hauling, and your topsoil stays put.

Request an estimate Call 254-279-0808

Clear the land without tearing it up.

A forestry mulcher chews brush and trees into a layer of mulch right where they stand. There are no piles to burn, nothing to haul off, and the ground isn't scraped bare the way it is after a dozer goes through.

The mulch holds moisture, keeps the soil from washing and breaks down over time. For pasture, hunting land, trails and fence lines it's often the better choice. We match the machine to the job, from light brush to heavy timber.

  4. MulchBrush and trees ground in place.
  5. Walk it againMake sure it looks the way you wanted.

What affects the price

Every property is different, so we price after we've seen it. These are the things that move the number.

  • DensityLight brush goes fast. Thick cedar and hardwood take longer.
  • Tree sizeBigger trees need a bigger machine.
  • AcreageLarger tracts usually cost less per acre.
  • TerrainSlopes, creeks and rocks slow the work.
  • Selective vs. fullWorking around keeper trees takes more care.

Mulcher or excavator?

Mulching is great for pasture, hunting land, trails and fence lines, and it's a good answer during a burn ban since there's nothing to burn. But it leaves stumps at ground level and roots in the soil.

If you're building a house pad, a slab or a road, the roots need to come out, so that ground gets cleared with an excavator instead. Lots of jobs use both: mulch the acreage, dig out the build site. We'll tell you which fits.
